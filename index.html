The timeline from first drip to repair

  1. Minute 0

    Stop the water

    Open the cabinet and find the two small valves on the wall or floor, one for hot and one for cold. Turn both clockwise until they stop. If the leak is behind the refrigerator, there is often a separate small valve for the ice maker line on the wall behind it or under the sink. If no valve works, close the main shutoff for the house.

  2. Minute 2

    Clear the cabinet and catch the drips

    Pull out cleaning products, trash bins, and anything stored below. Set a shallow pan or baking dish under the wettest spot and lay towels on the floor in front of the cabinet.

  3. Minute 5

    Figure out if it is supply or drain

    With the valves closed, does it keep dripping? Then the supply side, a valve, or the fridge line is likely. If it only leaks when you run the sink or the dishwasher drains, look at the drain pipes, the P-trap, and the disposal.

  4. Minute 10

    Look, but do not take things apart

    With a flashlight, check for a wet compression nut, a cracked braided supply hose, drips from the bottom of the disposal, or water running down from the faucet base. Snap a few photos. Hand-tightening a slip nut on the drain is fine; forcing anything is not.

  5. Minute 15

    Call the dispatch line

    Ring (229) 367-1193. Albany GA Plumbing Pros writes up what you found and books a plumber working near your street.

  6. Before the visit

    Prep the space

    Leave the cabinet empty, move the fridge out a few inches only if it slides easily, and keep pets out of the kitchen. Note the make of the disposal or dishwasher if you can see it.

  7. After the repair

    Dry it out properly

    Leave cabinet doors open with a fan pointed inside for a day or two. If the base has swollen or smells musty, it may need replacing once the plumbing is sorted.

Common kitchen leak sources: appliances, disposals, and pipes

Supply lines and valves

Braided hoses and old angle stops can weep or burst. A leak here drips constantly, even with nobody using the sink.

The P-trap and drain joints

Slip nuts loosen over time, and plastic traps can crack. These only leak when water is draining.

The garbage disposal

Leaks from the sink flange, the dishwasher inlet, or the bottom of the unit itself, which usually means internal seals have failed.

Dishwasher connections

Water at the air gap, a loose drain hose, or a supply fitting under the dishwasher that drips out onto the floor.

Refrigerator line

Thin plastic ice maker tubing can split. Some callers find water "flowing behind the fridge" long before they see a puddle.

The faucet

Worn seals at the base or a leaking sprayer hose send water straight down into the cabinet.

Small habits that prevent the next leak

  • Every few months, run a dry paper towel around the supply fittings and trap joints. Any damp spot is an early warning.
  • Do not store heavy items against the drain pipes, since bumping them loosens slip nuts.
  • Run cold water while the disposal is on, and avoid fibrous peels and grease.
  • When you pull the fridge out to clean, check the ice maker tubing for kinks or cracks.
  • Exercise the shutoff valves once or twice a year so they turn when you need them.

Kitchen leak questions

Can I keep using the sink if the leak is small?

If it only leaks while draining, you can usually use a different sink until the repair. If it drips with the valves open and nothing running, keep the valves closed, because supply leaks do not stop on their own.

The valve under my sink will not turn. What now?

Older valves can seize. Do not force them, since they can snap. Close the main shutoff for the house instead, and mention the stuck valve when you call so it can be replaced.

Water is coming from behind my refrigerator. Is that a plumbing call?

Often, yes. The ice maker or water dispenser line and its valve are plumbing parts. Shut that line off if you can find its valve, and have a plumber check the connection.

My dishwasher leaks only during the drain cycle. Why?

That points to the drain hose, the air gap, or the connection at the disposal or sink tailpiece. A partial clog in the kitchen drain can also push water out of the air gap.

Will a leak under the sink damage my floor?

It can if water sits for days. Cabinet bases absorb water quickly. Dry the area, keep the doors open, and ask about drying help if the floor beneath feels soft.
